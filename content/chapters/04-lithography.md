## Beat 4.1

A chip is made by printing. You shine light through a stencil onto the wafer, and where
the light lands, chemistry happens. Do that, layer over layer, dozens of times, and you've
drawn billions of microscopic switches.

The catch: to print features a few atoms' widths apart, you need light with an extremely
short wavelength — and producing it is barely possible. The machine that does it fires
50,000 droplets of molten tin per second, hits each one with a laser to create a flash of
plasma many times hotter than the sun's surface, and steers the resulting light with the
smoothest mirrors ever made. The machine is the size of a bus, costs a few hundred million
dollars, and ships to customers in 13 containers.

## Beat 4.2

Exactly one company on Earth can build it: ASML, headquartered in Veldhoven, a Dutch town
most people couldn't place on a map. Every advanced AI chip in existence passed through
an ASML machine. There is no plan B, and building one would take a competitor a decade or
more.

## Beat 4.3

Even ASML is an assembly point for other monopolies. The mirrors come from one German
optics house, Zeiss — polished so precisely that if a mirror were scaled to the size of a
country, its largest imperfection would be a fraction of a millimeter. The light source
comes from its San Diego operation. Optics modules from Connecticut. Single points of
failure, stacked.

## Beat 4.4

Companies that can print at the leading edge: one. Remember the count.
