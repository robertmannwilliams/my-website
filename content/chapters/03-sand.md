## Beat 3.1

Spruce Pine, North Carolina. Population: a few thousand. On a ridge above town sit two
mines that produce nearly all of the world's ultra-high-purity quartz — the material for
the crucibles in which every silicon chip's life begins. When Hurricane Helene flooded
this valley in 2024, people who track such things held their breath globally.

## Beat 3.2

Companies that can supply this material at scale: two. Ridges: one.

## Beat 3.3

Chips need silicon of absurd purity — better than 99.999999999% pure. Eleven nines. At
that standard, finding an impurity atom is like finding a dozen specific people among
all humans who have ever lived. A handful of plants in Germany, the United States, Japan,
and Korea refine it, growing the purified silicon into gleaming single-crystal ingots.

## Beat 3.4

The ingots are sliced into wafers: dinner-plate discs polished so flat that, scaled to
the size of a continent, the tallest bump would be millimeters high. Each wafer becomes
a few dozen finished AI chips.

## Beat 3.5

Five companies — led by two Japanese firms, Shin-Etsu and SUMCO — make roughly ninety
percent of the world's wafers. We're one step in, and the count of indispensable
companies is already in the single digits. It gets narrower.
