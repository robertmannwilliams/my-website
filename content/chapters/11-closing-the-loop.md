## Beat 11.1

Back to where we started. You type a question. What actually happens: your words are
converted to numbers and pushed through that giant file — the model — which predicts its
answer one word at a time, each word requiring a sweep through billions of stored
numbers. This is called inference, and it's why the buildings keep multiplying: training
a model is a one-time cost, but answering a few billion questions a day, forever, is the
real electricity bill. The two seconds feel free. Somewhere, a meter is running.

## Beat 11.2

And so the loop closes. Your question touched — in a real, physical, supply-chain sense —
a ridge in Appalachia, a refinery in Bavaria, crystal growers in Japan, a machine shop in
the Dutch countryside, mirror polishers in Germany, fabs on Taiwan's coast, memory towers
in Korea, a sandwich press in Chiayi, an assembly line in Houston, a campus in West
Texas, and a restarted nuclear plant in Pennsylvania.

Two seconds. Five continents. A handful of companies.
