## Beat 4.1

For twenty years, American electricity demand barely moved. Efficiency
swallowed growth — better bulbs, better motors, a flat line. Around 2023 the
line bent upward and kept going: data centers, new factories, electric cars.
The machines from the last story on this site all plug into this one. The grid
now has to do something it hasn't done in a generation: grow.

## Beat 4.2

The growth is already waiting in line. More than two terawatts of proposed
plants — mostly solar, wind, and batteries; more capacity than the entire
existing fleet — sit in interconnection queues, waiting for permission to plug
in. The typical wait runs to years. Building the plant is the easy part now.

## Beat 4.3

The bottleneck is the wires. A major transmission line takes ten years or more
— a couple to engineer, the rest to permit, across every county and courtroom
on its path. The grid's next decade will be decided less by physics than by
paperwork. The machine knows how to grow. The open question is whether it will
be allowed to.

## Beat 4.4

Right now — as you read this — the machine is doing everything you just
watched: clearing auctions every five minutes, holding sixty, carrying a
continent's evening on a few thousand humming plants. This isn't history, and
it isn't a forecast. It's the permanent present tense of the grid.
